A small old photo darkroom of 1980 in a Soviet photo studio, seen at eye level from about two metres inside the doorway. The only light is a red safelight bulb hanging from the ceiling, as used for developing film, and it gives every surface a deep red glow. Strings stretched across the room carry strips of film negatives hanging from clothespins to dry. Along the left wall stand metal shelving racks full of brown glass bottles, white enamel developing trays, boxes of photographic paper and round metal developing tanks. In the middle of the room stands a worn wooden work table with an enlarger, a mechanical timer, steel tongs and shallow trays of liquid, and in a clear patch of the table in front lies a stack of black-and-white photographic prints face up. The walls are dull dark green oil paint to chest height with bare plaster above, the floor is old stained linoleum, and a rubber apron hangs on a hook by the door. Empty room, no people. Photorealistic, 35mm lens, eye level, wide enough to show the table and the shelves, sharp focus from front to back, muted red film colors.
